There's no fixed number — it really depends on the tree's age, species, and growing conditions.

A few rough benchmarks:

  • Young tree (3-5 years old): maybe 10-50 apples
  • Mature dwarf tree: 100-300 apples
  • Mature standard-size tree (full-grown, well-established): 500-1,000+ apples, sometimes even up to 1,500-2,000 in a great year

Factors that swing this a lot: pruning, pollination, weather during bloom, thinning practices (many growers remove excess young fruit so the tree isn't overloaded), and the specific variety.

Similar ballpark to apples, with slight differences:

  • Young pear tree (3-5 years): maybe 10-40 pears
  • Mature dwarf pear tree: 50-150 pears
  • Mature standard-size pear tree: 300-700+ pears in a good year, occasionally more for very large, well-established trees

Pear trees tend to produce a bit less per tree than apple trees on average, partly because pears are often grown with wider spacing and lighter fruit set (growers commonly thin them too, to avoid limb breakage and get bigger fruit).
